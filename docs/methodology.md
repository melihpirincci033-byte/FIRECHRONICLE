# FIRECHRONICLE — Scientific Methodology

## Research Question

How can differences between MODIS and VIIRS active fire observations be accounted for to improve the consistency of long-term global fire activity analysis?

## Working Hypothesis

Differences in sensor characteristics and observation conditions contribute to inconsistencies in raw satellite fire detection counts.

A carefully designed spatial-temporal harmonization and calibration approach may improve their comparability.

## Planned Approach

**1. Data Acquisition:** Collect NASA FIRMS MODIS and VIIRS observations.

**2. Data Quality Assessment:** Examine missing values, geographic coordinates, observation dates, product-specific flags, and potential data quality issues.

**3. Spatial-Temporal Alignment:** Aggregate observations into common geographic cells and temporal intervals.

**4. Sensor Comparison:** Compare observations during periods in which MODIS and VIIRS are both available.

**5. Calibration:** Develop candidate methods to reduce sensor-dependent differences while preserving meaningful temporal variation.

**6. Validation:** Evaluate results using held-out observations and assess methodological uncertainty.

## Important Scientific Limitations

- Satellite hotspots are not equivalent to individual wildfire events.
- Different satellites may observe the same fire at different times.
- Spatial aggregation alone does not eliminate sensor differences.
- FRP and confidence values require sensor-specific interpretation.
- A harmonized activity indicator should not be presented as an exact count of fires.

## Validation Plan

Candidate methods will be evaluated using temporal and geographic holdout datasets.

Evaluation metrics and calibration choices will be finalized after exploratory analysis of the actual observations.

## Current Status

Methodology planning in progress. Calibration results have not yet been produced.
